### Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions
**356 upvotes** · Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04199)

Instead of calling a large remote LLM for every inference request, "compile by training" lets you describe a text function in natural language, then distill it into a small, local neural adapter trained on teacher-generated examples. The compiled function runs independently of the teacher models, can be versioned and composed like software, and achieves 83.6% semantic accuracy on FuzzyBench-Hard — a benchmark where the fast (seconds-long) compiler alternative scored 0% exact matches. Compile time is ~1 minute, a worthy tradeoff for teams wanting to eliminate repeated API costs and latency. Live demos include a multi-site web assistant and a bidirectional English↔Claudish translator.

### Terminal-Universe: Turning Agent Trajectories into Scalable Terminal Environments
**276 upvotes** · Jie Wu, Zhenru Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04148)

Post-training code agents requires executable environments, not just frozen trajectories — Terminal-Universe solves this by reconstructing environments directly from the file-operation history embedded in existing agent trajectories. From public trajectory data it produces **37,300 task-sufficient environments**, then scales them across breadth (cross-workspace queries spanning multiple codebases) and depth (multi-round iterative sessions). Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B on this corpus yields **+11.9 points** on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (single-round) and **+13.8 points** on EvoCode-Bench v2 MT@4 (multi-round) — meaningful gains from recycled data rather than expensive ground-up generation.

### LLaDA-Image: Building Strong Image Generators with Fully Open Training Recipes
**228 upvotes** · Chuyan Chen, Haoxing Chen et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03796)

LLaDA-Image pairs a 6B Diffusion Transformer trained from scratch with a frozen vision-language module built on the LLaDA2.0-Mini diffusion language model, producing photorealistic images that follow fine-grained text edits. Its pipeline trains on 220M samples (98% real images), uses parameter-free RMSNorm and the Muon optimizer for efficiency, and achieves **state-of-the-art among open-source models** on Qwen-Image-Bench (53.53 EN / 53.38 ZH). A distilled variant, LLaDA-Image-Turbo, enables fast inference in just 2–4 sampling steps. Weights, training code, and full recipes are publicly released.

### There's No Limit to How Bad Code Can Get
*2026-09-06* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/theres-no-limit-to-how-bad-code-can-get/) · tags: `migrations`, `technical-debt`

Simon's Lobste.rs comment pushes back hard on the "burn it down and rewrite" impulse: the old system becomes a moving, barely-maintained target while the new team underestimates the undocumented scope of what they're replacing, often resulting in **two broken systems instead of one**. His practical recommendation: add automated tests to the legacy codebase first, then attempt targeted refactors — and if a full migration is truly necessary, follow Will Larson's *Migrations: the sole scalable fix to tech debt*. Timely advice as AI-assisted greenfield rewrites become ever more tempting.
