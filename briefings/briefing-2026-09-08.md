## 🔬 Research

### Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions
**376 upvotes** · Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04199)

Instead of calling a large remote model at inference time for every input, "compile by training" converts a natural-language description of a text function into a small, self-contained neural adapter trained on teacher-generated examples. The result is a versioned, composable artifact that runs locally with no provider dependency and no per-call cost. On FuzzyBench-Hard (where the fast "Program-as-Weights" compiler got 0% exact matches), this approach reaches 83.6% semantic accuracy — at the cost of ~1 minute of compile time instead of seconds. The paradigm is compelling for production pipelines where latency, cost, and auditability matter.

### Knowing When Not to Reuse: Conditional Experience Transfer in Autonomous LLM Post-Training
**150 upvotes** · Tingyun Li, Wenfeng Feng et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26730)

Automated post-training pipelines reuse past successful updates to avoid redundant training runs, but an update that worked on a previous model checkpoint may be harmful after subsequent training has shifted the base model. This paper formalises the problem as *conditional experience transfer* and proposes BCIT (Boundary-Calibrated Intervention Transfer), which checks whether a past update's context still holds before authorising its reuse. Tested on a 4B model across finance reasoning, text-to-SQL, and function calling, BCIT authorises fewer harmful updates and achieves better final model quality under equal compute budgets. Directly relevant to anyone building continuous or agentic fine-tuning systems.

### Dr. Claw: An AI Scientist Workspace for Vibe Research
**120 upvotes** · Dingjie Song, Hanrong Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00365)

Dr. Claw is an open-source orchestration layer (AGPL-3.0) that wraps existing coding-agent executors (Claude Code, Gemini CLI, etc.) in a human-in-the-loop research workflow rather than replacing them with a new autonomous agent. It introduces persistent state objects, a reusable skill library, and multi-executor coordination to keep planning, execution, and writing in one traceable, recoverable loop. Evaluated against a bare command-line agent sharing the same backend, the orchestration layer alone improves research completeness while producing an auditable process trail. It targets the real pain point of AI-assisted research: decisions and failures are rarely preserved for review.
