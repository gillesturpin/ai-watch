## 🔬 Research

### NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness
**194 upvotes** · NeoHorse Team (Guoliang Cao, Guohao Dai, Tianyu Guo, Kai Han) · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08183)

NeoHorse-1 is a family of agent-native models that implements a concrete feedback loop for recursive self-improvement: it routes each user turn to a model tier, records the interaction, validates and labels the trace, then feeds it back as training data for the next iteration. The system closes an evaluation-selection-update loop where what the model learns to do directly shapes its next training mixture. Post-training lifts macro-average benchmark scores from 58.94 → 64.87 at 4B and 65.60 → 69.04 at 9B, notably shrinking the gap between the post-trained 4B and the raw 9B base. This is one of the more concrete prototypes of automated curriculum generation from live deployment signals, a direction increasingly relevant as labs look beyond static training sets.

### Unlocking Lossless Speedups in LLMs via Discrete Diffusion
**127 upvotes** · Subham Sekhar Sahoo et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04010)

The paper introduces **Uno**, diffusion-augmented LLMs that keep standard AR weights but add lightweight diffusion weights trained to generate multiple tokens in parallel — no separate draft model needed, unlike speculative decoding. A new sampler family (Ψ-Spec) guarantees lossless acceleration and supports inference-time scaling at fixed context length. The 8B Uno model achieves up to **3× throughput** over its AR base and outperforms both the 26B DiffusionGemma and proprietary Mercury 2 on agentic tool use, coding, and long-context reasoning benchmarks. Code and checkpoints are publicly released, making this a practically actionable speedup for any team running open-weight AR models at scale.

### AuK Technical Report: An Open-Source Foundational Model for Speech Generation and Editing
**117 upvotes** · Ziyang Ma et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.08936)

AuK is an open-source unified speech model covering generation, content editing, enhancement/separation, paralinguistic editing, and acoustic editing — all driven by natural-language instructions. It was trained on ~3.03 billion instruction-audio pairs and 1.95 million hours of supervision, combining a multimodal LLM, a jointly-trained VAE (speech + audio + music), and a hybrid rectified-flow Transformer. A distilled variant, **AuK-Flash**, runs in 4 inference steps without classifier-free guidance, achieving a **4.5× wall-clock speedup** over the full model. Both weights and code are released, positioning AuK as a strong open alternative to proprietary speech APIs for teams building voice products.

## 📡 Analysis

### On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem
*2026-09-08* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) · tags: `mathematics`, `ai`, `openai`, `generative-ai`, `llms`, `training-data`, `ai-ethics`

OpenAI's unreleased model resolved the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness Millennium Prize Problem (~$1M prize) in **88 hours** after launching agents on September 1st, using 2.7 million messages and ~300 billion output tokens. The result is overshadowed by a serious ethics dispute: NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge had been working on the same problem for nearly a year using Claude and Codex, had a breakthrough on August 15th, and allege OpenAI launched its effort only after rumors of their work circulated — with unresolved questions about whether their Codex sessions were used in training. The episode crystallizes two compounding concerns: AI-accelerated scooping of human researchers, and the opacity of what goes into frontier model training data.

### Quoting Terence Tao
*2026-09-09* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/9/terence-tao/) · tags: `ai-ethics`, `mathematics`, `ai`

Fields Medalist Terence Tao warns that AI is causing a "non-renewable mining" of good open mathematical problems: the mere rumor that someone is working on a problem now triggers a wave of AI-powered attempts to solve it first, before the original researchers can complete their work. He argues this dynamic is pushing researchers toward secrecy, potentially reversing centuries of open-science norms. Read alongside the Navier–Stokes controversy above, this points to a structural tension between AI speed and the collaborative, credit-based ecosystem that produces hard mathematical progress.
